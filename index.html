How to play
  • Left thumb moves you. Push the stick to its edge to run. Running leaves scratch marks the hunter can follow; ease off to walk, or crouch to stay hidden at range.
  • The big button on the right does whatever is in reach: repair, heal, unhook, vault a window, drop or vault a pallet, open a gate. Moving cancels it.
  • When a ring appears while you repair or heal, tap as the needle crosses the marked zone. Miss and the generator bangs, and the hunter hears it.
  • A heartbeat means the hunter is within 32 metres. A red glow on the ground is the hunter about to come round the corner.
  • Two hits put you down. Three hooks, or too long on one, and you are gone. Friends on hooks and on the ground show through walls.
  • Premonition is your team's shared guess at where the hunter is: red haze on the ground and a red arc around you that narrows as the guess sharpens.
  • The eye at the top is the hunter's guess about you. When it opens wide, move.
  • Each map has its own job and its own kind of exit; the map picker says what to do, and the line at the bottom-left of the screen always shows the next step. Each map has its own memory.
  • Sprinter, Medic, Survivalist and Trickster get an extra button above Crouch for their ability. The Necromancer uses the big button next to a fallen teammate's cross. Teammates play a random mix of the other roles.

Both sides learn only from finished trials, and only from what they could have seen or heard. On a keyboard: WASD, Shift to walk, Space to act, C to crouch.

    Room code

      Training complete

      That is everything a trial asks of you: repair, vault, drop pallets, lose the hunter, save your friends and get out. A real trial is the same, with less time to think.

      A practice trial is a normal one on Ironworks Yard, set to a single dull-witted Warden and veteran teammates. You can change any of that on the menu afterwards.

      Getting better

      • Walk whenever you hear a heartbeat. Scratch marks are how most chases start.
      • Generators first. Every second you spend hiding with nothing chasing you is a second of repair lost.
      • Aim for the bright edge on skill checks: each great one is nearly a second of free repair.
      • Lead a hunter round a window or a pallet, not across open ground. Save the pallet until it is right behind you.
      • Heal out of the hunter's sight, and go for an unhook early instead of leaving it to the last seconds.
      • Watch the eye. If it opens wide while you cannot see the hunter, it has worked out where you are: move.
      • The hunter remembers the routes you take. If you always run to the same corner, pick another.
